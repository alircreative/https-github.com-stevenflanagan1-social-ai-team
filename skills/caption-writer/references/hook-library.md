# Hook Library

*Reference file for the /caption-writer skill. Use these hook formulas to open captions with a line that stops the scroll. Never use the same hook type twice in a row across a batch of captions.*

---

## How to Use This Library

A hook is the first line of a caption. It is the only line most people will read. It determines whether someone taps "more" or keeps scrolling.

Rules for effective hooks:
- Be specific — vague hooks underperform. "This changed everything" is weak. "This one email got us 47 replies in 3 hours" is strong.
- Create an information gap — hint at something useful, surprising, or counterintuitive without giving it away
- Match the hook to the post's objective: curiosity hooks for educational content, social proof hooks for testimonial posts, story hooks for behind-the-scenes
- The hook must deliver on its promise — a hook that does not connect to the content creates distrust

---

## Curiosity Hooks

These hooks create an information gap — the reader feels like they are missing something and clicks through to find out what.

**1. The counterintuitive fact**
"[Widely believed thing] is wrong — here's what actually works."
*Example:* "Posting every day is killing your engagement — here's what actually works."
*Use when:* You have genuine evidence that contradicts conventional wisdom. Do not use for mild or obvious takes.

**2. The hidden truth**
"Most [target audience] don't know this about [topic]."
*Example:* "Most small business owners don't know this about Instagram's algorithm."
*Use when:* You are sharing a genuinely non-obvious insight. Overused — only use if the insight is specific enough to justify it.

**3. The surprising number**
"[Specific number] [unit] to [surprising outcome]."
*Example:* "4 minutes a day to cut customer churn by 23%."
*Use when:* You have a real, specific statistic. Do not use vague or invented numbers — your audience will fact-check.

**4. The thing no one talks about**
"Nobody in [industry/niche] talks about [topic]. I'm going to."
*Example:* "Nobody in the fitness industry talks about what happens when you stop losing weight. I'm going to."
*Use when:* You are addressing a taboo, uncomfortable truth, or genuinely underexplored topic.

**5. The cliffhanger setup**
"[Context-setting statement] — what happened next surprised everyone, including us."
*Example:* "We launched a product with zero paid ads, no influencer budget, and no PR. What happened next surprised everyone, including us."
*Use when:* You have a story with a genuine unexpected outcome. The story must deliver — do not bait with "what happened next" and then underdeliver.

**6. The open loop**
"I almost [dramatic negative outcome]. Here's the one thing that stopped it."
*Example:* "I almost shut down the business in March. Here's the one thing that stopped it."
*Use when:* Personal brand posts, behind-the-scenes, case studies. Creates immediate personal stakes.

**7. The myth bust**
"[Stated as obvious fact]: [common belief]. Reality: [truth]."
*Example:* "Widely believed: you need a huge following to make sales. Reality: 200 engaged followers outperform 20,000 passive ones every time."
*Use when:* You can back up the debunk with evidence or experience. The contrast format works especially well on Instagram and LinkedIn.

**8. The provocative claim**
"[Specific, contestable claim that your target audience cares about]."
*Example:* "The best marketing strategy for small businesses in 2025 is one most of them refuse to try."
*Use when:* You want engagement (replies, saves, shares). The claim must be specific enough to provoke — not so general it produces nodding, not so extreme it loses credibility.

**9. The number list tease**
"[Number] things I wish I knew about [topic] before I [started/spent/invested/tried]."
*Example:* "5 things I wish I knew about Instagram Reels before I wasted 6 months posting the wrong content."
*Use when:* Educational posts, particularly carousels or video. The specificity of the number and the regret framing are both important.

**10. The unexpected admission**
"I was doing [thing] wrong for [time period]. Here's how I found out."
*Example:* "I was pricing my services wrong for two years. Here's how I found out."
*Use when:* Personal brand posts, case studies, or any post where vulnerability will build trust. The admission must be genuine.

**11. The future stakes**
"In [timeframe], [prediction about the industry or audience's situation]."
*Example:* "In 12 months, every brand that's not using short-form video will feel it in their reach numbers."
*Use when:* Thought leadership, trend content, educational posts with urgency. Works well on LinkedIn and X.

## Contrarian / Challenge Hooks

These hooks push back on something the audience already believes, which creates friction and engagement.

**12. The direct challenge**
"Stop [common action the audience does]. Do this instead."
*Example:* "Stop writing captions that start with your brand name. Do this instead."
*Use when:* You have a clear, actionable alternative to offer. The challenge must have immediate value — do not challenge without providing the replacement.

**13. The unpopular opinion**
"Unpopular opinion: [specific, contrarian take]."
*Example:* "Unpopular opinion: engagement rate matters more than follower count at every stage of business growth."
*Use when:* You want debate-style engagement. Only use if you are prepared to defend the opinion with evidence in the caption body.

**14. The "you've been lied to" hook**
"You've been told [incorrect advice]. Here's what actually happens when you follow it."
*Example:* "You've been told to 'find your niche'. Here's what actually happens to most people who follow that advice."
*Use when:* You are debunking popular advice with a genuine alternative. The strongest version of this hook names the specific bad advice rather than being vague.

**15. The "everyone else is wrong" hook**
"Every [role/type of person in your industry] is doing [thing] wrong. Here's what the data actually shows."
*Use when:* You have data or evidence. Bold claims need receipts — do not use without supporting content.

**16. The permission-giver**
"You are allowed to [thing the audience feels guilty about or uncertain about]."
*Example:* "You are allowed to charge more. You are allowed to say no. You are allowed to rest."
*Use when:* Your audience faces self-doubt or social pressure around a topic. Works especially well for personal brand posts and coaching content.

**17. The "what they don't tell you"**
"What nobody tells you about [topic]."
*Example:* "What nobody tells you about growing a service business past six figures."
*Use when:* You have a genuine inside-track perspective. Overused in online business spaces — only use if the "what nobody tells you" is actually specific and genuinely underreported.

**18. The reframe**
"[Common thing] is not [what everyone thinks it is]. It's actually [reframe]."
*Example:* "Consistency is not posting every day. It's showing up every week for years."
*Use when:* You want a quote-style hook that is likely to be saved and shared. Keep the reframe tight and memorable.

**19. The hot take**
"[Blunt statement of opinion that some will agree with and some won't]."
*Example:* "Most 'authentic' brand social media is still just marketing in different clothes."
*Use when:* You are comfortable with mixed reactions. The best hot takes generate both strong agreement and strong disagreement — both drive engagement.

## How-To Hooks

These hooks promise practical value and are most effective for educational content.

**20. The specific promise**
"How to [specific outcome] in [specific timeframe or condition]."
*Example:* "How to write a caption that converts in under 10 minutes."
*Use when:* You can deliver exactly what you promise. Do not overpromise.

**21. The reverse how-to**
"How NOT to [common mistake]. (And what to do instead.)"
*Example:* "How NOT to write a call-to-action. (And what to do instead.)"
*Use when:* Leading with the mistake is more engaging than leading with the correct approach. Works well for audiences who have experienced the frustration firsthand.

**22. The simple system**
"The [X]-step system for [outcome] that [your target audience] can actually use."
*Example:* "The 3-step system for planning a month of content that busy founders can actually use."
*Use when:* Your audience feels overwhelmed and wants something manageable. The "actually" framing signals accessibility.

**23. The shortcut**
"The fastest way to [desired outcome] is not [what they think]."
*Example:* "The fastest way to grow your Instagram following is not posting every day."
*Use when:* You have a counterintuitive shortcut to share. The most effective version names the common (wrong) approach.

**24. The beginner frame**
"If I were starting [thing] from scratch today, here's exactly what I'd do first."
*Example:* "If I were starting a social media presence from scratch today, here's exactly what I'd do first."
*Use when:* Experience-based educational content. Works well for established accounts sharing their process retrospectively.

**25. The tool/resource reveal**
"The [tool/resource/approach] that [specific result]. (Most people overlook it.)"
*Example:* "The free tool that cut our content creation time in half. (Most people overlook it.)"
*Use when:* You are sharing a specific recommendation. The specific result and "most people overlook it" both add intrigue.

**26. The expert shortcut**
"Experts in [field] do [thing] differently. Here's the part they never explain."
*Use when:* You can genuinely explain the expert-level nuance that is missing from most beginner content. Do not use if the "expert secret" is actually common knowledge.

**27. The process reveal**
"Exactly how we [achieved specific outcome] — with all the mistakes included."
*Example:* "Exactly how we grew this account from 0 to 12,000 followers in 8 months — with all the mistakes included."
*Use when:* Transparency-based educational content. The "all the mistakes included" framing differentiates it from a highlight reel and signals honesty.

## Social Proof Hooks

These hooks lead with results, testimonials, or credibility signals.

**28. The result-first hook**
"[Client/customer name or descriptor] [achieved specific outcome] in [timeframe]. Here's how."
*Example:* "A hairdresser in Bristol doubled her bookings in 6 weeks. Here's what changed."
*Use when:* You have a real, specific client result you can share (with permission). The more specific the result and the more relatable the client, the better this performs.

**29. The credibility signal**
"After [time/volume/experience], here's what I've learned about [topic]."
*Example:* "After writing over 2,000 social media captions, here's what I've learned about what actually stops the scroll."
*Use when:* Establishing expertise on educational content. The number should be real and specific.

**30. The before/after**
"[Describing the "before" state with real emotion]. Now [describing the after]."
*Example:* "She was spending 6 hours a week on content that got 12 likes. Now she spends 2 hours and books 3 new clients a month from it."
*Use when:* Transformation stories, case studies. The "before" must feel genuinely painful or frustrating — not just "things were a bit hard."

**31. The social validation**
"[X number of people/businesses] have used this [approach/tool/method] to [outcome]. Here's why it works."
*Use when:* You have real volume data to cite. Do not invent numbers.

**32. The testimonial tease**
"'[Direct quote from a client/customer that captures a real, specific result or feeling]'"
*Example:* "'I went from dreading Mondays to looking forward to them.' Here's what changed for [client descriptor]."
*Use when:* You have a genuine, specific testimonial. Direct quotes outperform paraphrasing for social proof content.

**33. The milestone**
"[Milestone] reached. Here's what got us here — and what we'd do differently."
*Example:* "10,000 followers reached. Here's what got us here — and what we'd do differently."
*Use when:* Milestone posts. The "what we'd do differently" framing transforms a self-congratulatory post into something genuinely useful.

## Story / Personal Hooks

These hooks open with a scene, a moment, or a personal admission that pulls the reader in emotionally.

**34. The scene-setter**
"[Time, place, and one specific sensory or emotional detail]. That was the day I learned [lesson]."
*Example:* "It was 11pm and I was rewriting the same email for the fourth time. That was the day I learned how much time I was wasting on perfectionism."
*Use when:* Behind-the-scenes posts, origin stories, personal brand posts. Specific sensory details make the scene real.

**35. The moment of change**
"[Date or time marker]. [Something specific changed, went wrong, or began]."
*Example:* "Three years ago this week, I sent an invoice for the first time. I didn't sleep until the client replied."
*Use when:* Milestone posts, reflection posts, personal brand anniversary content.

**36. The failure admission**
"I got this completely wrong."
*Use when:* You are sharing a genuine learning from a mistake. The hook is only three words — the caption must deliver the full story clearly.

**37. The dialogue opener**
'"[Direct quote from a real conversation, client, or inner voice]"'
*Example:* '"Why would anyone follow a small local business?" My business mentor asked me that in 2021. Here's how I answered — and what I've learned since.'
*Use when:* Story posts that start from a real conversation. The dialogue makes the reader feel they are overhearing something.

**38. The confession**
"I have a confession: I [thing the audience might also do or think but not admit]."
*Example:* "I have a confession: I spend more time on the first line of a caption than on everything else combined."
*Use when:* Relatability-first content. The confession must be something the audience genuinely recognises in themselves.

**39. The time pressure**
"[Timeframe from now]. If you [action or inaction], [consequence]."
*Example:* "Six months from now. If you have not started building an email list, you will regret it."
*Use when:* Creating urgency around an evergreen piece of advice. The consequence must be real and relevant — not manufactured urgency.

**40. The honest assessment**
"I am going to be honest about [topic] because no one else seems to be."
*Use when:* Opinion-led content where the brand has a strong point of view. The follow-through must be genuinely honest and specific, not just edgy.

## Question Hooks

These hooks address the reader directly with a question they want answered.

**41. The "are you doing this?" check**
"Are you making this [mistake/assumption] about [topic]?"
*Example:* "Are you making this mistake with your Instagram hashtags?"
*Use when:* Educational content where the audience is likely doing something suboptimally. The question creates personal stakes.

**42. The aspirational question**
"What would you do with [desired outcome] per [time period]?"
*Example:* "What would you do with 10 extra hours per week?"
*Use when:* Promotional or problem-agitate-solution posts. Sets up the aspiration before introducing the solution.

**43. The diagnostic question**
"Why is your [thing the audience has] not [performing/working/growing] the way it should?"
*Example:* "Why is your content calendar not translating into actual results?"
*Use when:* Diagnostic content that identifies the problem and then solves it.

**44. The empathy question**
"Do you ever feel like [relatable frustration the audience experiences]?"
*Example:* "Do you ever spend an hour writing a caption that gets seven likes?"
*Use when:* Empathy-first posts that validate the audience's frustration before providing the solution.

**45. The curiosity question**
"What actually happens when you [take the action you are recommending]?"
*Example:* "What actually happens when you post consistently for 90 days?"
*Use when:* Leading into a case study, results post, or "the truth about" educational post.

**46. The direct ask**
"[Specific question that your audience has a strong opinion on]?"
*Example:* "What is the first thing you do when writing a caption?"
*Use when:* Community engagement posts, poll-style posts, or any post designed primarily to generate comments.

## Quick Reference: Which Hook for Which Post Type

| Post Type | Best Hook Categories |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Educational tip | How-To, Curiosity, Contrarian |
| Behind the scenes | Story/Personal, Curiosity |
| Social proof / testimonial | Social Proof, Story/Personal |
| Product feature | How-To, Curiosity, Social Proof |
| Promotional offer | Curiosity, How-To, Question |
| Community / engagement | Question, Contrarian |
| Personal brand opinion | Contrarian, Story/Personal |
| Milestone / announcement | Social Proof, Story/Personal |
| Thought leadership | Contrarian, Curiosity |
