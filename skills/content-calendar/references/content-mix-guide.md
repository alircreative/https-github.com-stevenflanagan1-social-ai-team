# Content Mix Guide

*Reference file for the /content-calendar skill. Use this to determine the recommended content pillar ratios for a given brand type, then adjust based on best-performers data if available.*

---

## How to Use This Guide

1. Identify the brand's business type from `context/brand-style.md` (industry, products/services, goals)
2. Match to the closest business type below
3. Apply the recommended pillar ratios to the total number of posts for the month
4. Round to whole numbers — a ratio is a guide, not a rule
5. If `context/best-performers.md` exists and shows a particular pillar consistently outperforming, shift up to 10 percentage points toward that pillar, and reduce the lowest-performing pillar accordingly
6. Always state the ratios you're using before building the calendar

## Business Types and Recommended Ratios

### E-commerce / Product Brands

Brands that primarily sell physical products — fashion, beauty, food and beverage, home goods, consumer electronics, gifts.

| Pillar | Recommended % | Description |
|--------|--------------|-------------|
| Product / Promotional | 40% | Product features, new arrivals, limited editions, offers, sales, gift guides, product use cases |
| Educational | 30% | How-to content, styling guides, ingredient explainers, care guides, buyer's guides, "what to look for when buying X" |
| Behind the Scenes | 20% | Packaging, production, sourcing, team, warehouse, the brand story |
| Community / UGC | 10% | Customer features, reposts, reviews, user questions and answers |

**Notes for E-commerce:**
- Promotional content over 40% causes follower fatigue — use hooks that lead with value before the ask
- Educational content with product integration outperforms direct product posts on awareness-focused accounts
- Behind-the-scenes content builds trust for premium-priced products especially
- If the brand has a strong UGC stream, consider increasing community content to 20% and reducing promotional to 30%

---

### Service Businesses

Brands that sell expertise, time, or outcomes — marketing agencies, law firms, accountants, coaches (not personal brands — see below), cleaning services, trades, healthcare providers, real estate.

| Pillar | Recommended % | Description |
|--------|--------------|-------------|
| Educational / Tips | 40% | Practical tips, "how to" content, explainers, myth-busting, guides — establishes expertise |
| Social Proof / Case Studies | 30% | Client results, testimonials, before/after outcomes, process walkthroughs |
| Behind the Scenes | 20% | Team, the working process, day-in-the-life, tools and methods |
| Promotional | 10% | Service announcements, offers, availability, how to work together |

**Notes for Service Businesses:**
- Educational content is the core trust-builder — do not reduce it below 30%
- Social proof must be specific to perform — "we helped a client save money" is weak; "we cut a client's tax bill by $14,000 in their second year" is compelling
- Promotional content at more than 15% typically causes service businesses to plateau in follower growth
- Behind-the-scenes humanises the people delivering the service — important for high-ticket services where trust is critical

---

### Personal Brands / Coaches

Individuals building an audience around their expertise, perspective, or methodology — business coaches, life coaches, fitness coaches, consultants, speakers, authors, creators.

| Pillar | Recommended % | Description |
|--------|--------------|-------------|
| Educational | 40% | Teaching content, frameworks, actionable tips, explainers drawn from the individual's expertise |
| Personal Story | 30% | Failures, lessons, behind-the-scenes of life/business, opinions, experiences, values |
| Promotional | 20% | Course, programme, book, service — always anchored in value, not a sales pitch |
| Community | 10% | Audience questions answered, responses to comments, polls, shoutouts |

**Notes for Personal Brands:**
- The personal story pillar is what differentiates a personal brand from a generic educational account — do not treat it as optional
- Promotional content at 20% is high — it works for personal brands because audiences follow people, not companies. But the promotional posts must feel generous, not grabby.
- Educational content should always feel like the brand's unique perspective — not generic tips that could come from anyone
- If the person has a book or flagship course, one promotional post per week during launch phases is acceptable

---

### Restaurants / Hospitality

Cafes, restaurants, bars, hotels, event venues, food trucks, catering companies.

| Pillar | Recommended % | Description |
|--------|--------------|-------------|
| Food / Atmosphere | 40% | Hero food photography, drinks, plating, ambience, seasonal specials, the space |
| Behind the Scenes | 30% | Kitchen prep, chef stories, sourcing and suppliers, staff, events setup |
| Promotional / Offers | 20% | Bookings, events, specials, seasonal menus, vouchers, limited-time offers |
| Community | 10% | Customer celebrations (birthdays, anniversaries, milestones), local partnerships, reviews |

**Notes for Restaurants/Hospitality:**
- Visual quality is everything in this category — every image should look edible or aspirational
- Behind-the-scenes content that shows the care and craft in food preparation is among the highest-performing content for restaurants on Instagram and TikTok
- Reels and video content significantly outperform static in this category — weight the content calendar toward video formats
- Promotional content over 25% risks making the account feel like a flyer, not a destination

---

### B2B Companies

Companies that sell to other businesses — SaaS, technology, manufacturing, professional services, logistics, wholesale, recruitment.

| Pillar | Recommended % | Description |
|--------|--------------|-------------|
| Thought Leadership | 40% | Industry insights, opinion pieces, trend analysis, commentary on sector news, predictions |
| Educational | 30% | How-to content, guides, explainers, product use cases, best practices in the industry |
| Social Proof | 20% | Case studies, client wins, partnership announcements, awards, testimonials |
| Company Culture | 10% | Team, values, hiring, events, company milestones |

**Notes for B2B:**
- LinkedIn is almost always the primary platform — write the content calendar with LinkedIn as the lead platform and adapt to others as secondary
- Thought leadership must express genuine opinions — vague or hedged "insights" are ignored
- Product-forward content performs poorly in B2B social unless it is tightly tied to a specific problem it solves
- Company culture content is the most humanising content type for B2B — do not skip it even if it feels off-brief
- B2B audiences are smaller but more valuable per follower — engagement rate benchmarks are lower than B2C, but that is expected

## General Rules for Any Business

These rules apply regardless of business type:

**1. No two consecutive posts in the same format.**
A feed of 3 static posts in a row tells the algorithm (and the audience) that content is predictable. Vary Reel → Carousel → Static → Reel → Static, etc.

**2. No two consecutive posts with the same hook type.**
Do not open two posts in a row with questions, or two in a row with statistics. Vary the approach every post.

**3. Promotional content should never open with the offer.**
Every promotional post should lead with a value statement, a problem, or a story — and arrive at the offer after earning the reader's attention.

**4. Adjust ratios based on the funnel stage.**
- New accounts (under 1,000 followers): reduce promotional to 5–10%, increase educational and social proof to grow trust first
- Established accounts (10,000+): can sustain higher promotional ratios as the audience already trusts the brand
- Launch periods: can temporarily increase promotional to 25–30% for 2–4 weeks, then return to baseline

**5. Platform matters.**
The same content mix ratios can apply across platforms, but the format and length must be platform-native:
- Instagram: weight toward Reels and Carousels
- LinkedIn: weight toward thought leadership and educational long-form posts
- TikTok: weight toward behind-the-scenes and entertainment formats
- Facebook: weight toward community and conversational posts
- Threads / X: weight toward opinion and educational short-form posts

**6. When best-performers.md exists, trust the data over the guide.**
These ratios are starting points for a new brand with no data. Once a brand has 2–3 months of performance data, the best-performers log takes precedence. If educational content is performing at 4x the rate of promotional content for a specific brand, increase educational content regardless of what the guide recommends.
