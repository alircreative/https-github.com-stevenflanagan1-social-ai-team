# Social Media Engagement Benchmarks

*Reference file for the /social-performance-review skill. Use these benchmarks to assess whether a post, an account, or a platform is performing well, on par, or below expectations. Benchmarks are industry averages — treat them as a guide, not a verdict.*

*Sources: aggregated from Hootsuite, Sprout Social, Rival IQ, and Later industry benchmark reports. Updated for 2024–2025.*

---

## How to Use These Benchmarks

- Compare the brand's actual metrics against the range for their account size and platform
- "Good" means performing above the average for that tier — something worth understanding and replicating
- "Needs investigation" means consistently falling below average — worth identifying the cause
- A single underperforming post is noise. A pattern of underperformance is a signal.
- If you have competitor data (from Firecrawl), compare directly — competitor benchmarks are more relevant than industry averages

## Instagram

**Engagement Rate Formula:**
`(Likes + Comments + Saves + Shares) ÷ Reach × 100`

Note: Instagram's native engagement rate uses reach, not followers. Posts boosted with paid reach will show lower organic engagement rates — flag this when comparing.

### By Account Size

| Account Size | Average Engagement Rate | Good | Excellent | Needs Investigation |
|-------------|------------------------|------|-----------|---------------------|
| Under 1,000 | 5–8% | 8%+ | 12%+ | Under 3% |
| 1,000–5,000 | 4–7% | 7%+ | 10%+ | Under 2.5% |
| 5,000–10,000 | 3–5% | 5%+ | 8%+ | Under 2% |
| 10,000–50,000 | 2–4% | 4%+ | 6%+ | Under 1.5% |
| 50,000–100,000 | 1.5–3% | 3%+ | 5%+ | Under 1% |
| 100,000+ | 1–2% | 2%+ | 3.5%+ | Under 0.8% |

### By Format

| Format | Average Engagement Rate | Notes |
|--------|------------------------|-------|
| Reels | 3–6% | Reach is typically 2–5x higher than static posts — high reach can deflate engagement rate even on good content |
| Carousels | 4–7% | Highest save rates of any format — saves indicate high-value educational content |
| Static images | 2–4% | Declining format — static posts typically reach fewer non-followers |
| Stories | 3–5% (sticker interactions / reach) | Use taps forward/back and exits as quality signals alongside engagement |

### Key Instagram Metrics to Track

| Metric | What Good Looks Like | Notes |
|--------|---------------------|-------|
| Saves (per post) | 1–3% of reach | Saves indicate the content is reference-worthy — a strong signal for educational content |
| Comments (per post) | 0.5–1.5% of reach | Genuine comments > emoji comments — track quality, not just count |
| Shares (per post) | 0.3–1% of reach | Shares drive new reach — a strong share rate on a post is worth investigating for the formula |
| Story completion rate | 70%+ | Under 60% suggests stories are too long or not immediately engaging |
| Profile visit rate | 5–10% of reach | How many people who saw the post visited your profile — indicates interest beyond the post |

### Follower Growth Benchmarks (Instagram)

| Account Size | Typical Monthly Growth | Strong Growth |
|-------------|----------------------|---------------|
| Under 5,000 | 2–5% | 8%+ |
| 5,000–20,000 | 1–3% | 5%+ |
| 20,000–100,000 | 0.5–1.5% | 3%+ |
| 100,000+ | 0.3–0.8% | 1.5%+ |

## Facebook

Facebook organic reach has declined significantly since 2019. Benchmarks reflect current organic performance without paid boost.

**Engagement Rate Formula:**
`(Likes + Comments + Shares + Reactions) ÷ Reach × 100`

### Reach Rate (organic)

| Account Size (Page Likes) | Typical Organic Reach Rate | Notes |
|--------------------------|---------------------------|-------|
| Under 1,000 | 20–35% | Small pages reach a higher proportion of their audience |
| 1,000–10,000 | 10–20% | |
| 10,000–50,000 | 5–12% | |
| 50,000–100,000 | 3–6% | |
| 100,000+ | 1.5–4% | Large pages have proportionally low organic reach — paid amplification is expected |

### Engagement Rate (of reach)

| Metric | Average | Good | Excellent |
|--------|---------|------|-----------|
| Overall engagement rate | 1–3% | 3–5% | 5%+ |
| Comments | 0.1–0.5% of reach | 0.5%+ | 1%+ |
| Shares | 0.2–0.8% of reach | 0.8%+ | 1.5%+ |

### Link CTR (Facebook)

| Post Type | Average CTR | Good |
|-----------|-------------|------|
| Link posts | 0.5–1.5% | 2%+ |
| Video with link in caption | 0.3–0.8% | 1%+ |

**Facebook-specific notes:**
- Native video (uploaded directly, not YouTube link) reaches 3–5x more than external links
- Posts that ask questions in the caption get 2–3x the comment rate of declarative posts
- Emotional content (celebration, empathy, nostalgia) consistently outperforms informational content on Facebook
- Best performing days: Wednesday and Thursday, 10am–3pm (local time)

## LinkedIn

LinkedIn benchmarks differ significantly from other platforms because the feed is feed-driven (not follower-driven) and content can resurface for days after posting.

**Engagement Rate Formula:**
`(Likes + Comments + Reposts) ÷ Impressions × 100`

Note: LinkedIn uses impressions (not reach) — this naturally lowers the engagement rate figure vs platforms using reach.

### By Account Type

| Account Type | Average Engagement Rate | Good | Excellent |
|-------------|------------------------|------|-----------|
| Personal profile | 2–5% | 5–8% | 10%+ |
| Company page | 0.5–2% | 2–4% | 5%+ |

Personal profiles dramatically outperform company pages on LinkedIn. If the brand has a key founder or executive with a LinkedIn profile, personal content typically produces 5–10x the reach of identical content on a company page.

### Key LinkedIn Metrics

| Metric | Average | Good | Notes |
|--------|---------|------|-------|
| Impressions per post (personal) | 500–5,000 | 5,000+ | Varies enormously by network size and content type |
| Impressions per post (company page) | 200–2,000 | 2,000+ | |
| Comment rate | 0.3–1% | 1%+ | Comments on LinkedIn have outsize algorithmic value — they extend post life significantly |
| Repost rate | 0.1–0.5% | 0.5%+ | Reposts drive distribution to second-degree network |

### Follower Growth (LinkedIn)

| Profile/Page Size | Typical Monthly Growth |
|------------------|----------------------|
| Under 1,000 | 3–8% |
| 1,000–5,000 | 1–4% |
| 5,000–20,000 | 0.5–2% |
| 20,000+ | 0.3–1% |

**LinkedIn-specific notes:**
- Posts published Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday between 8am–11am (local time) consistently outperform other days
- Text-only posts with no images or links frequently outperform image posts — the algorithm does not penalise lack of visual for personal profiles
- First comment from the author (adding context or starting a question) in the first 30–60 minutes significantly boosts reach
- Long-form articles (LinkedIn Articles) drive newsletter subscriptions but much lower engagement than feed posts — track separately

## X / Twitter

X metrics are impressions-based. Engagement rate is calculated on impressions, not followers.

**Engagement Rate Formula:**
`(Likes + Replies + Reposts + Bookmarks + Link clicks) ÷ Impressions × 100`

### By Account Size

| Followers | Average Engagement Rate | Good | Notes |
|-----------|------------------------|------|-------|
| Under 1,000 | 3–6% | 8%+ | Small accounts with engaged niches can see very high rates |
| 1,000–10,000 | 1.5–4% | 5%+ | |
| 10,000–50,000 | 0.8–2.5% | 3%+ | |
| 50,000–100,000 | 0.5–1.5% | 2%+ | |
| 100,000+ | 0.3–0.8% | 1.5%+ | |

### Key X Metrics

| Metric | What Good Looks Like | Notes |
|--------|---------------------|-------|
| Impressions per post | Varies widely by account size and content | Compare to your own baseline more than industry average |
| Reply rate | 0.2–1% of impressions | Replies indicate the post sparked conversation — high-value signal |
| Bookmark rate | 0.1–0.5% | Bookmarks = saved-for-later, similar to Instagram saves |
| Link CTR | 0.5–2% | Highly variable by content type and CTA strength |
| Repost rate | 0.3–1% | Reposts are the primary distribution mechanism on X |

### Link CTR (X)

| Post Type | Average CTR | Good |
|-----------|-------------|------|
| Link with preview | 0.5–1.5% | 2%+ |
| Link without preview (text only) | 1–2.5% | 3%+ |
| Thread with link at end | 1.5–3% | 4%+ |

**X-specific notes:**
- The first reply to your own post (adding context, a resource, or a question) significantly increases engagement
- Images attached to posts typically increase impressions by 30–40%
- Poll posts have the highest engagement rates but lowest link CTR — good for community building, poor for traffic
- Best performing times: 8–10am and 12–1pm (local time), Monday–Thursday

## Threads

Threads is relatively new and benchmarks are still maturing. Treat these as early-stage indicators.

**Engagement Rate Formula:**
`(Likes + Replies + Reposts) ÷ Impressions × 100`

Note: Threads shares data with Instagram's backend — impressions include algorithmic reach beyond followers.

### Engagement Rate

| Account Size | Average Engagement Rate | Good | Notes |
|-------------|------------------------|------|-------|
| Under 5,000 | 4–10% | 12%+ | Platform is still rewarding new accounts generously |
| 5,000–20,000 | 2–6% | 8%+ | |
| 20,000–100,000 | 1–4% | 6%+ | |
| 100,000+ | 0.5–2% | 4%+ | |

### Key Threads Metrics

| Metric | What Good Looks Like | Notes |
|--------|---------------------|-------|
| Likes per post | 1–5% of reach | Lower than Instagram — Threads audience is scroll-heavy |
| Replies per post | 0.3–2% | Replies are the primary engagement metric — Threads rewards conversations |
| Reposts | 0.1–0.5% | |

**Threads-specific notes:**
- Threads content is not indexed by search outside the platform — discoverability is algorithmic, not search-based
- Reply chains (commenting on your own posts, engaging with replies) significantly extend post life
- There is no native scheduling tool for Threads yet — most third-party schedulers work via the API
- No hashtag performance data is reliable yet — the consensus is that hashtags do not materially boost reach on Threads

## TikTok

TikTok metrics are primarily view-based. Engagement is measured against views (not followers), which reflects the non-follower-centric nature of TikTok's For You Page algorithm.

**Engagement Rate Formula:**
`(Likes + Comments + Shares + Saves) ÷ Views × 100`

### By Account Size

| Followers | Average Engagement Rate | Good | Excellent |
|-----------|------------------------|------|-----------|
| Under 1,000 | 8–15% | 15%+ | 25%+ |
| 1,000–10,000 | 6–12% | 12%+ | 20%+ |
| 10,000–50,000 | 4–8% | 8%+ | 15%+ |
| 50,000–100,000 | 3–6% | 6%+ | 10%+ |
| 100,000+ | 2–5% | 5%+ | 8%+ |

### View Benchmarks

| Account Size | Average Views per Video | Strong Performance |
|-------------|------------------------|-------------------|
| Under 1,000 followers | 200–2,000 | 5,000+ |
| 1,000–10,000 | 1,000–10,000 | 25,000+ |
| 10,000–50,000 | 5,000–50,000 | 100,000+ |
| 50,000+ | 10,000–100,000+ | Viral threshold varies widely |

### Video Completion Rate

| Rate | What It Means |
|------|--------------|
| 25% | Minimum threshold — many viewers dropped off early |
| 50% | Average — half of viewers watched half the video |
| 75%+ | Strong — most viewers are watching most of the video |
| 90%+ | Excellent — algorithm will favour this video significantly |

**TikTok-specific notes:**
- TikTok completion rate is the single most important signal for the algorithm. A 15-second video with 90% completion rate will outperform a 60-second video with 30% completion rate.
- The first 1–3 seconds of a video determine whether it will perform. Front-load the hook.
- For TikTok, view-through rate (percentage who watched to the end) is more valuable than like rate
- Sound-on is the norm on TikTok — unlike other platforms where most viewers watch silently. Audio hooks matter.
- Best posting frequency: 3–5 videos per week minimum for growth. Consistency matters more than any other factor.
- Profile traffic from TikTok tends to be lower than Instagram but follow-through rate (viewers who follow) is higher when content resonates

## What "Good" Looks Like vs What Is Worth Investigating

### Signs of a Healthy Account (even if individual posts vary)

- Engagement rate is above average for account size at least 50% of the time
- Top-performing posts are notably better than the average (2–3x) — this means the content mix includes "hero" content
- Follower growth is positive month over month, even if slow
- Save rate on educational content is above 1% of reach (Instagram) — this is a strong signal of genuine value
- Comment quality: comments address the content specifically, not just emoji reactions

### Signs That Need Investigation

- Engagement rate is below the "needs investigation" threshold for 3 or more consecutive weeks
- Follower count declining — this usually means content is not meeting expectations of the existing audience
- Reach declining despite consistent posting — this typically means the content is getting low completion rates (video) or being shared to small non-engaged audiences
- Comments dropping to near zero — post is reaching an audience that is not engaging
- High reach but low engagement — often indicates boosted content reaching untargeted audiences, or content that is not compelling enough to act on

### Red Flags That Warrant a Content Strategy Review

- Every format is underperforming (not just one type) — suggests a voice or positioning problem, not a format problem
- Educational content outperforms promotional content by 5x or more — the brand may be building an audience that is not buying, and the mix needs rebalancing toward conversion content
- Engagement from Reels is not converting to followers — indicates the content is entertaining but not compelling enough to follow
- Follower growth is strong but engagement rate is declining — the brand may be attracting the wrong audience or the content mix may not be serving the existing audience

### Seasonal and Contextual Adjustments

Always consider external factors before concluding that underperformance is a content problem:
- Public holidays reduce posting team activity AND audience activity
- News events in the same news cycle can suppress organic reach regardless of content quality
- Platform algorithm changes can cause sudden reach drops — check industry news before blaming the content
- Summer months (June–August) typically see lower B2B engagement; December sees lower engagement across most platforms in the final 2 weeks
